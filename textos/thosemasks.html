Those masks

I adopt a pearl white mask, a facade of who I am. I dive myself into turmoil, shadowing the dark that is concealed underneath the pearl white shell that does not define my inner self. I realize this, and then break the mask, molding myself towards something different. This difference, from a mask placed in front me, I take it and a beautiful red shell encases my inner world. I look back, the white mask undefined, and a new found pride acknowledges the progress that is, for now I am red of revelation, and the immature white is no more. The red shell encases my inner dark world, and conceals what is underneath. I realize this, and I escape to a new turquoise shell. I realize this again, and I break the mask. I see a rich lavender shell placed in front of me, one that shines over my dark monochromatic inner core. I continue this behavior, mask after mask, pieces of color, arranged in sympathetic patterns and shapes. Pieces of shell that create a glassy figure above my core, a print of the perspective that I consider elegance. An elegance that has evolved through years of pride and turmoil, of grace and experience. A unique pattern that molds into a uniform idea of what I present: myself, created myself. It is myself, for it is unique, thousands of colors shaped in unique patterns, elegance that only describes me and no one else. Yet, I cannot escape the defining factor of it all, the pieces that make such a personal art form, made from masks that come and go, never mine. Thousands of little pieces, little pieces of mask. The face that I created, born from my own perspective, unable to be recreated by none but myself. Yet is it truly mine? I result unknown, a dull grey underneath the plagiarized abstraction above.
